MDM vs Kimodo — generated motions

Side-by-side skeleton animations from an experimental comparison of two generations of motion-diffusion models: MDM (Tevet et al., 2022) and NVIDIA Kimodo (Rempe et al., 2026), evaluated on the Kimodo Motion-Generation Benchmark and on HumanML3D prompts. Representative cases per experiment (best / median / worst); numbers in panel titles are per-case metrics.

MDM    Kimodo    skate = foot-skate ratio (lower is better)

Experiment 1 — Text-to-motion

A shared prompt is given to both models. Cases are chosen per difficulty tier by the largest, median, and smallest quality gap. Watch the feet: MDM tends to glide even when nearly stationary; Kimodo plants and lifts.

Experiment 2 — Diversity (4 seeds at once)

Four samples of the same prompt drawn together (opacity distinguishes seeds). MDM's samples often scatter in mutually inconsistent directions; Kimodo varies path and style while keeping the commanded semantics.

Experiment 3 — Keyframe in-betweening

Both models must hit given keyframes. MDM (inpainting) shows velocity spikes at keyframe boundaries — visible as jerks or teleports; Kimodo's native conditioning transitions smoothly. Panel titles show the boundary-spike factor (× median motion speed).

Experiment 4 — Path following

The character must follow a prescribed root path. MDM satisfies the path exactly by construction but is dragged along it with skating feet; Kimodo walks it. The ground grid slides under the follow camera, making sliding feet visible.

Skeleton visualizations of model outputs only (22-joint common layout); no dataset motions are redistributed here. MDM outputs from the public motion-diffusion-model checkpoint; Kimodo outputs from Kimodo-SOMA-RP-v1.1 under the NVIDIA Open Model License. Benchmark: Kimodo Motion-Generation Benchmark.
